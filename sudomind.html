SudoMind

An Android Sudoku app built end to end — including a hint engine that solves the way a person would, so a stuck player learns a technique instead of being handed a number.

Overview

The problem

Most Sudoku apps hand you the correct digit when you ask for help. That ends the puzzle instead of teaching you how to get past it.

My approach

I wrote a hint engine around real solving techniques, then designed the whole app — progression, events, statistics, themes — as a product rather than a single screen.

Result

A complete, themeable app on its fifth iteration, now in release preparation with AdMob integrated.

What's in it

  • Technique-based hint engine — hints come from human solving strategies, so each one points at the reasoning rather than the answer
  • Daily challenges and streaks — a reason to open the app tomorrow, with progress that persists
  • Swipeable difficulty tabs — ViewPager2 navigation between difficulty levels
  • Animated how-to-play tutorial — onboarding that shows the rules instead of listing them
  • In-app events with live countdowns — timed events backed by persistent notifications
  • Statistics — solve times, completion rates and history per difficulty
  • Themes and settings — multiple visual themes and a full settings system
  • Custom win and game-over dialogs — designed screens rather than default alerts

Build notes

  • Making hints teach rather than solve

    The engine works out which technique applies to the current board and surfaces that, which meant implementing the solving strategies themselves instead of reading from a stored solution.

  • Keeping state consistent across sessions

    Streaks, statistics, event countdowns and notifications all have to agree with each other after the app is closed, reopened, or left alone for days.

  • A win dialog that silently refused to fire

    Tracked down to Windows line endings in a file the logic depended on — a good reminder that a bug making no sense in the logic is often not in the logic.
